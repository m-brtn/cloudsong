# Devlog — 2026-11-08 (Sunday)

## Goal of the day

The first demo game on Quench, made entirely by agents: a calm exploration of floating grassy isles at the golden
hour above a sea of clouds. A small hooded figure with a flowing cape walks, runs, jumps, glides and flies, gathers
drifting lights, lights candles, wakes spirits and brings them to a temple of light. One beautiful region, 10 to 15
minutes of play, on the Mac and the iPhone. Everything procedural (no third-party art).

## Problems and solutions

- **The clouds cannot be below y = 0.** The engine's weather clamps `cloudBase` to 0 and above. The isles float at
  y = 2200 m instead, above cloud bases at 620 m (cumulus about 1.5 km thick).
- **The sun dimmed and no clouds below the horizon.** The engine dimmed the sun by the cloud cover within 6 km of
  the camera, clouds below it included (0.7: the sun at about 30%), and its sky pass drew the clouds only above the
  horizon. Fixed in the engine (`Weather::coverAbove`; the clouds composited below the horizon over the cloud base;
  the 2D layer drawn from above): commit `4a22a0f`.
- **The fog hid the clouds.** The height fog's base 120 m under the isles, falloff 0.006/m: the air below them was
  opaque. Base at the isles, density 0.00035, falloff 0.0009.
- **The player slid off the hill.** Its slope reached about 46 degrees (Jolt's virtual character slides above 52 with
  the rolling noise added). The hill is wider and lower (radius 16 m, 7 m).
- **No numpy on the machine.** The sounds will be synthesized in C++ at run time instead of Python at build time.
- **The cape stood up over the head in a glide.** Logged numbers (`cape_up`, `cape_back` and a debug print of the
  forces) showed the hem 0.24 m above the collar: the glide sank at 4.3 m/s (gravity 15.5 against a 0.2 s approach
  to 1.45 m/s), so the air from below lifted the cape; and the cloth's damping per frame (0.985) acted as a strong
  drag at 120 frames per second. The glide now scales gravity by 0.1 (sink about 1.5 m/s), the damping works on the
  swing relative to the collar with a rate per second; the hem now trails 0.17 m below the collar, 0.5 m back.
- **The player could not climb the stair, and every slope slowed it down.** On the ground the vertical speed was
  kept at `min(v.y, 0)`: the fall speed built up while grounded and the contact with an upward slope turned it into
  a loss of speed (2.4 m/s of 4.6 up a 20-degree ramp); the stair's risers also caught the capsule. The ground speed
  is now horizontal only, and the stair's collision is a smooth ramp through the middles of the steps (the steps
  are drawn, not collided). On the stair 3.75 m/s forward and 1.3 m up in a second.
- **A debug assertion in Jolt (`AABBTreeToBuffer: Bounding box became smaller`).** The cave's crown ring had NaN
  positions: `pow(cos(pi / 2), 0.7)` with the float cosine slightly below zero. Clamped to zero; static meshes are
  also built around their middle (Jolt keeps a mesh's node bounds as half floats, coarse 2 km up) and skip
  degenerate triangles.
- **`quench_weather_sounds()` failed in the game's build** (`'/assets/make_weather_sounds.py' ... missing`): the
  engine read the script's directory from a variable of its own CMake scope, which a game does not see. Fixed in the
  engine (`56d0b8f`, a cache variable; engine devlog, 05:20); the game pins it.
- **The updraft held the player at about 3 m.** Its lift approached 9 m/s with a time constant of 0.6 s against the
  full gravity: they balanced at -0.36 m/s. The lift now carries the weight (gravity cancelled inside it) and pulls
  toward the column's middle.
- **The player could not climb the temple's tiers** (0.4 m steps; Jolt's stair walking did not lift the capsule over
  them, as on the stair). The tiers collide as a cone over their edges (23 degrees).
- **Space on the title did nothing.** With ImGui's keyboard navigation on, ImGui took the keyboard while the title's
  window was focused, and the engine's key bindings then read as up. Keyboard navigation is off (gamepad navigation
  stays on); Return and Space begin, Escape rests and resumes.
- **The temple's tiers showed grass on top.** `render::cylinderMesh` is open at both ends: the tops were holes onto
  the ground below. The tiers are lathed closed drums with a narrow bevel.
- **The closing card over the title.** `views.txt` went to the title after the end, and the card was drawn over every
  screen. It is drawn only while playing; the title is captured before the end.
- **`tools/package.sh --ios` looked for `Cloudsong.xcodeproj`.** The Xcode generator names the project after the
  CMake project (`cloudsong`), and the app after the target. Fixed; the script also passes an empty bundle id suffix
  (a paid team).

## Limitations and open questions

- The engine's cloud shadow map lies on the plane y = 0 (`docs/weather.md`): cloud shadows are off here.
- The cave's outside is still a smooth mossy dome (the boulders are gone). The isles' rims show as flat bands.
- The touch controls ran only under scripted fingers on the Mac; nobody has used them on a phone.
- Nobody has listened to the score or the sounds: they are checked by measurement only.
- The spirits fly in straight lines (through rock if it is in the way); the stair and the temple's tiers collide as
  ramps, so feet float or sink a little on the steps.
- The macOS builds use the local engine override; the iOS build used the pinned commit alone.
- Nothing ran on a phone. With the engine's measured ratio (an iPhone 17 about three times an M4 Pro's GPU time),
  Balanced at the phone's scale would take about 8 to 16 ms on the GPU, the meadow at the edge of 60 Hz: an estimate,
  not a measurement. The thermal governor lowers the render scale and the clouds when the phone grows hot.
- The offscreen times are not frame rates on a display; no on-screen benchmark was run (no windows on the screen
  in this work).
